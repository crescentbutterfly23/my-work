# VELUM — Photography direction

**The upward gaze.**

Velum is not a travel company: it is the parent house of six different businesses. So the
photography cannot be a catalogue of subjects — planes, bottles, buildings, containers — it has to
be **one way of looking**, applied to all of them.

That way of looking is the plane seen from below: the camera sits underneath, the subject is cut
against the light, and there is air all around it. A plane, a bottle, a crane, the corner of a
building or a car shot this way belong to the same brand even though they share no object at all.

> If a Velum photograph can be described without naming its sector, it is working.

---

## 1. The seven principles

### 01 · Low angle
The camera sits **below the subject and looks up**. This is not a decorative angle: it is the
position of someone looking at what lies beyond. Minimum 15° above the horizontal; the sweet spot
is 30–45°.

When the subject cannot be overhead (a plate, a document), it is replaced by its equivalent:
**the camera at surface level**, looking towards the horizon.

### 02 · Backlight
The light source sits **behind the subject**. The object falls to silhouette or near-silhouette and
the ground behind it — sky, wall, water, dust — carries the light. Expose for the highlights; let
the shadows fall towards blue and never lift them in post.

Where it appears, the rim of light on the subject is **warm**: it is the only gold in the frame.

### 03 · Air
Between **60% and 70% of the frame is empty**. The subject lives in the last third, almost always
off-centre, and the rest is space. The air is not leftover background: it is the subject.

### 04 · One subject
One object, one gesture, one silhouette. Never a three-product still life, never a collage. If two
elements are needed, one dominates and the other is barely implied.

### 05 · The threshold
Something in frame marks **a limit being crossed**: the horizon, the edge of a roof, the rim of a
cloud, the mouth of a bottle, a doorway, a shoreline, the edge of a slab. It is the veil made
literal. Without a threshold the picture is handsome but it is not Velum.

### 06 · Hard matter
Where there is ground or wall, it is **stone, concrete, asphalt, marble or metal** — the same
surfaces as `05_Textures`. Stone holds the expansive up: an unmovable surface, a limitless view.

### 07 · Colour
Shadows towards **Velum Blue `#0B1D34`**, highlights towards **Bone White `#F4F0EE`**, and **one
warm accent** per image — a flare of sun, a gold rim, a single amber object. No teal-and-orange,
no neon, no HDR.

## 2. Technical spec

| | |
|---|---|
| **Lens** | 24–35 mm for the low angle (lines converge, the sky opens). 85–135 mm for detail and horizon compression. No fisheye. |
| **Aperture** | f/8 – f/11 for sky frames: the subject stays crisp against the ground. f/2 – f/2.8 for close detail only. |
| **ISO** | The lowest the scene allows. Grain is added afterwards if wanted, never inherited from noise. |
| **Exposure** | Expose for the sky or for the light source. Losing shadow detail is accepted: the silhouette is the point. |
| **Hour** | Golden hour ±40 min and blue hour. Midday **only** when hard shadow on stone is the subject. |
| **Format** | 4:5 and 2:3 vertical for the upward gaze. 16:9 and 2:1 for horizons. 1:1 for social only. |
| **Delivery** | RAW + 16-bit TIFF. sRGB for digital, Adobe RGB for print. 4000 px minimum on the long edge. |

### Grading

- Blacks are **not pure**: the black point sits at Velum Blue, not `#000000`.
- Whites are **not blown**: the white point lives at Bone, not `#FFFFFF`.
- Soft curve, no exaggerated clarity, no HDR. One warm accent per image.
- Fine grain optional, equivalent to ISO 400 film. Never a vignette below −10.
- No replaced skies, no added flares in post.

## 3. What each house photographs

The grammar does not change; the subject does.

| House | Subject | The frame |
|---|---|---|
| **Velum Travel** | Aircraft, wing, contrail, control tower | Pure low angle against open sky. The parent reference. |
| **AL — Ana López** | Bottle, glass, jasmine | The bottle from below against a lit limewash wall; light passes through the liquid and leaves the gold. |
| **Velum Foods** | Raw ingredient, hands, table, market | Camera at the level of the stone table, light from behind, steam or flour dust catching the backlight. |
| **Velum Trade** | Crane, container, rope, dock | Low angle from the foot of the crane. Structure as architecture, not as logistics. |
| **Velum Properties** | Building corner, stair, doorway | The corner against the sky, lines converging. The doorway as a literal threshold. |
| **Velum Mobility** | Vehicle, charge point, open road | Low, almost at asphalt level, the car cut against the dawn. Road aggregate in the foreground. |

**Three frames per house** as a minimum on every shoot: one of **gaze** (low angle with air), one of
**matter** (detail on a hard surface), one of **threshold** (the limit being crossed).

## 4. What we look for and what we avoid

### We look for
- Clean silhouettes against open light
- Skies with structure: layers, contrails, high cloud
- Hard surfaces with real texture
- Scale: something small that reveals something enormous
- A single warm flare
- Frames with more air than feels comfortable

### We avoid
- **Planets, rockets, astronauts, galaxies, nebulae.** The celestial lives in the light and the
  horizon, never in the set dressing. This is the rule most often broken and the one that does the
  most damage.
- People posing, smiles to camera, "the office team", handshakes
- Generic corporate stock of any kind
- High saturation, teal-and-orange, neon, HDR, replaced skies
- Product collages and crowded still lifes
- Top-down drone shots: looking down is the opposite of this brand
- Flat white studio backgrounds

## 5. The logotype over photography

1. It goes **in the cleanest, darkest area** of the frame — usually the low sky or the top corner.
   Never over the subject.
2. **One-ink version**: `velum-primary-blanco` over a dark image, `velum-primary-azul` over a light
   one. Never the two-colour version over a photograph.
3. If the ground gives no contrast, **do not add a box**: change the frame, or use a soft gradient
   of Velum Blue itself at 40–60%.
4. Keep the full «x» clear space, even when the air in the picture invites you to move closer.
5. An image carries **a logotype or a line of text, not both** fighting for the same space.

---

## 6. Briefing a shoot

What a photographer gets, on one page:

> Camera low, looking up. Light behind the subject. One object. Two thirds air. Something marking a
> limit: a horizon, an edge, a doorway. Stone or concrete underfoot if there is ground. Blue
> shadows, bone highlights, one gold accent. No posed people, no planets or rockets, no HDR.

And the three required frames: **gaze, matter, threshold.**

## 7. Generated imagery

When an image is generated rather than photographed, exactly the same guide applies.
`Prompts/prompts-en.md` holds the base prompt, the six per-house variants and the negative prompt.

Rules specific to generated material:

- Check for **deformations** in hands, text, windows, wheels and reflections.
- Never generate **text** inside the image: type is set afterwards, in Cinzel and Montserrat.
- Never generate **recognisable people** or third-party logos.
- File the prompt beside the image in `References/`, so the series can be repeated.
- `References/INDEX.md` catalogues every approved image with the principle and house it demonstrates. A reference with no entry in the index is not a reference yet.
- A generated image is never presented as documentary photography of a real company asset: if it is
  a Velum Travel aircraft, photograph the aircraft.
